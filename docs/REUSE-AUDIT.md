# Reuse audit for norwegian-laws-history

Draft for [history issue #1](https://github.com/sondreskarsten/norwegian-laws-history/issues/1), reviewed 25 September 2026. This is a component decision and migration proposal, not a claim that historical reconstruction is ready.

## Decision

Reuse the repaired acquisition and cache mechanics. Extract the snapshot integrity and rendering mechanics only behind explicit contracts. Rewrite legal-time interpretation and the ordered structural verifier. Retire the synthetic history builder, its output graph, and the old force-push workflow from the new product.

The first product is an **observed-source history**: what immutable source bytes were available to this collector at a recorded time. It must not present an observation date, Git timestamp, tag label, or best-effort SQLite date as a legal commencement date. No old `law-history` commit, tag, generated legal text, or synthetic baseline enters the new repository.

## Shared-code extraction boundary

Keep the repositories as distinct products. `norwegian-laws` remains the source acquisition/current-reader producer; `norwegian-laws-history` consumes immutable evidence and publishes observation history. Do not make the history product import publisher UI modules, SQLite display projections or `lovdata_loader.reconstruct` at runtime.

The immediate shared boundary is a **versioned artifact interface**, not a new service or a third repository. Pin producer commit, parser source hashes, dependency lock and content/formatter contracts. Once stabilized, pure archive identity, manifest validation, normalized-event and canonical-render utilities may be extracted as versioned leaf modules in the existing producer project. A released package or exact-commit dependency is acceptable; an editable moving-branch dependency is not. Preserve its MIT notices and test provenance when extracting code.

History-owned modules should be small: evidence reader/validator; immutable observation ledger; independent structural verifier; explicit temporal claims; and Git tree materializer. The producer remains responsible for raw acquisition and the complete parsed interface. The publication workflow supplies durable evidence publication and consumer readback. The history consumer uses that delivery boundary without a second acquisition pipeline.

## Conditions before reuse and migration

1. Land the producer evidence contract and pin its exact commit/schema; verify public receipts against the supported interface rather than treating local integration as publication evidence.
2. Start the history repository from its own README/contract commit. Record an acquisition made now, with raw evidence and attribution. Do not manufacture observations for 2001 or for a source publication timestamp.
3. Accept raw observations only after complete artifact/member reconciliation. Keep parser failure and unsupported shape records; they must not disappear from inventories.
4. Introduce the bounded ordered grammar and fail-closed verifier. Raw evidence may be accepted when parsing fails; a parsed or formatted text may not be promoted as verified.
5. Materialize a small observed pilot and a second real observation, using the observation cutoff and unchanged canonicalizer. If source content is unchanged, report that; do not manufacture a legal change.
6. Add legal-state reconstruction only in later work with operation-level evidence, resolved scope/commencement and independently checked prior states. The first pilot makes no such claim.

## Remaining unknowns

Independently verified full-corpus structural coverage, archive member completeness under every upstream shape, operation-level commencement, and a replay-complete historical baseline are not established. No removal from the current archive proves a repeal date. The Bokmål/Nynorsk duplicate Constitution identity requires preservation of both source occurrences; the current last-occurrence selection policy is a current-product rule, not the history identity rule. Long-term evidence retrievability and fork execution must be verified by the publication workflow before claiming delivery.
